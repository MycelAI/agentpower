Horsepower Then,
Agentpower Now

How AI tokens became the fuel of a cognitive Industrial Revolution, and what the original one teaches us about what happens next.

By Alphin Tom · Mycel UG · CC BY-SA 4.0
Scroll
The Definition

One agentpower (AP) equals the total cognitive output of one knowledge worker, working full-time for one year, without AI assistance.

In 1782, a Scottish engineer named James Watt needed to sell steam engines to brewery owners. He had a problem. The brewers didn't understand pressure differentials or thermal efficiency. They understood horses.

So Watt did something that changed the world more than any of his mechanical innovations. He measured how much work a horse could do, and then he invented a word for it: horsepower. One steam engine, he told the brewers, produces the equivalent of twenty horses. No horses to feed, no stables to maintain, no rest days. Just coal and fire and output.

The brewers bought the engines.

Two hundred and forty-four years later, I'm watching something eerily similar unfold. Not in a brewery, but everywhere people sit at desks and think for a living. The fuel isn't coal anymore. It's AI tokens. And the work being amplified isn't physical. It's cognitive.

We don't have a word for it yet. I think we need one.

IThe Old Formula

If you run a company, any company, there's a number you know by instinct even if you've never written it down. It's the rough relationship between what you pay people and what they produce.

For most of the 20th century, and the first two decades of the 21st, a healthy knowledge-work business operated somewhere around a simple ratio: for every dollar or euro you spent on an employee, you generated roughly two in revenue. Sometimes 1.8, sometimes 2.5, but the band was narrow. The economics were remarkably consistent. Revenue tracked headcount almost linearly. Want more output? Hire more people. Want to grow faster? Hire faster.

This formula shaped everything. How we structured organisations. How we valued companies. How we planned budgets. How we designed entire national economies.

The old production function
Output ≈ Labour

More hands, more output. Proportionally, predictably, linearly.

That formula is breaking.

IIThe New Fuel

In February 2026, a fintech company called Klarna published its annual numbers. They deserve to be read slowly.

Klarna 2022 → 2026
Revenue growth+104%
Revenue per employee3.6×
Remaining employee pay raise+60%
Headcount−49%

Half the people. More than double the revenue. Higher pay for the ones who remain.

What happened? Klarna became one of the first large companies to systematically replace departing staff not with new hires, but with AI. Task after task shifted from human labour to AI systems. The fuel for those systems? Tokens, purchased from AI providers for a fraction of what the equivalent human labour would have cost.

Klarna isn't an anomaly. It's a signal. AI-native companies now generate over $4 million per employee. Industries most exposed to AI are growing revenue per employee at three times the rate of those less exposed. The acceleration is compounding.

The new production function
Output ≈ Labour × Fuel

The fuel is AI tokens. And just like coal in the 1780s, the fuel is cheap relative to the labour it amplifies.

IIIWhat a Token Actually Is

If you work outside the technology industry, "AI tokens" probably sounds like jargon from someone else's world. It isn't.

A token is a small unit of text, roughly three-quarters of a word. When you interact with an AI system, everything you send is broken into tokens, and everything it sends back is also tokens. Every contract it drafts, every spreadsheet it analyses, every report it summarises, every calculation it checks: all measured in tokens.

AI companies sell access to their systems by the token. The prices have been falling at a speed that has no precedent in the history of technology. A million tokens, enough to process roughly 750,000 words, costs a few dollars. Not a few hundred. A few.

The cognitive equivalent of a full working day of drafting, summarising, analysing, and responding to routine queries can be fuelled by tokens costing less than a cup of coffee. Not a metaphorical cup of coffee. A literal one.

This is the coal of our era. Abundant, cheap, and capable of transforming the economics of any work that involves humans thinking, writing, analysing, and communicating. Which is most work.

IVThe Measurement Problem

Here is where the story becomes about measurement. And measurement is where revolutions are either understood or wasted.

When Watt invented horsepower, he didn't discover anything new about physics. He created a translation layer between the world people understood and the world that was arriving. That act of naming gave decision-makers the conceptual tools to think clearly. Buy the engine or keep the horses? At twenty horsepower, the answer was obvious.

We're at the same juncture. And right now, we're measuring the wrong thing.

In March 2026, the CEO of Nvidia told a podcast audience that he'd be "deeply alarmed" if an engineer earning $500,000 didn't consume at least $250,000 worth of AI tokens. Days later, an employee at Meta created a leaderboard ranking over 85,000 employees by token consumption. The top user burned through 281 billion tokens in a month. Engineers competed to climb the board, a phenomenon now called "tokenmaxxing." Some left AI systems running idle for hours to inflate their numbers. The leaderboard was taken down within days.

This is the equivalent of measuring a factory's productivity by how many tonnes of coal it burns. It tells you nothing about what was produced. It incentivises waste. It confuses fuel consumption with useful work.

The right measure isn't tokens consumed. It's cognitive work produced per human operator.

VAgentpower

I want to propose a word. And a definition precise enough to outlast this essay.

Agentpower (AP): One unit of agentpower equals the total cognitive output of one knowledge worker, working full-time for one year, without AI assistance.

That's the baseline. 1 AP is what one person produces in a year unaided: the contracts drafted, the patients seen, the reports written, the calculations run, the decisions made. The cognitive equivalent of one horse pulling a mill wheel.

A knowledge worker operating with AI tools doesn't produce at 1 AP. Depending on the work and the skill of the operator, they produce at 3 AP, 5 AP, 8 AP, sometimes 10. The cognitive equivalent of three, five, eight, ten unaided professionals.

The AP Ratio
AP Ratio = Total Cognitive Output ÷ Human Headcount
AP Ratio Scale
Pre-2023 baseline1.0 AP
Early AI adoption2.0 AP
Klarna (2026)3.5 AP
Deep integration5.0 AP
Expert operator (Meta, per CTO)10.0 AP

The word "agent" carries a double meaning that makes it precisely right. In the old world, an agent is a person who acts on behalf of another. In the new world, an "agent" is an AI system that acts autonomously to complete tasks. The word bridges both eras.

And here is the distinction that matters most. Agentpower measures the combined system, not the AI alone. The human is the operator. The AI is the engine. Without the human's judgement, the engine produces noise. Without the engine, the human is limited to 1 AP. The power is in the pairing.

VIThis Is Not a Technology Story

There's a tempting narrative that this matters only to the technology industry. That narrative is the most dangerous misunderstanding of this decade.

It's the same mistake people made about the steam engine in 1800, when it was "just" a mining pump. Steam didn't stay in the mines. It moved into textiles, where mechanised spinning increased output per worker by a factor of 500. It moved into iron production, where output scaled a thousandfold. By 1870, one steam horsepower equalled the labour of 21 manual workers, and Britain's total installed steam capacity was equivalent to 43 million human labourers in a country of 31 million people.

Steam replaced muscle. Tokens amplify mind.

Every profession built on reading, writing, analysing, summarising, calculating, diagnosing, advising, planning, drafting, or communicating sits inside the blast radius of this shift.

LawDrafting briefs, reviewing case files, summarising precedent
MedicineInterpreting scans, differential diagnosis, referral letters
EngineeringStructural calculations, compliance checks, specifications
FinanceUnderwriting, anomaly detection, scenario analysis
ConsultingDesk research, deliverables, proposal drafting
ArchitectureDesign iteration, specification writing, code review
EducationLesson planning, assessment grading, curriculum design
AdministrationApplication processing, logistics, communications

This doesn't mean replacement. It means amplification. The professional who learns to operate at 5 AP becomes five times more formidable. But the professional who stays at 1 AP while their peers move to 5? In the coal age, horse-powered factories didn't close overnight. They closed gradually, then suddenly.

VIIThe Cost of One Agentpower

New Model · 5 AP
$90K
1 salary + token fuel / year

The human component remains. You still need the operator. The best operators become more valuable, because their judgement directs the engine.

But the additional agentpower, the 2nd through 8th AP per person, comes from tokens costing $3,000 to $6,000 per worker per year. That is not an incremental improvement. It's a structural collapse in the cost of cognitive work.

By the mid-19th century, steam cost 25 to 30% as much as the equivalent horse power. The factories that adopted steam didn't just become more efficient. They made the horse-powered competition structurally unviable. The parallel is uncomfortable precisely because it's exact.

VIIIThe Engels' Pause

Now the chapter that intellectual honesty demands and optimism alone cannot write.

For the first fifty years of the Industrial Revolution, roughly 1780 to 1840, nearly all productivity gains flowed to capital owners. Real wages for workers stagnated or declined. This period is known as the "Engels' Pause," after Friedrich Engels, who documented the misery of industrial workers even as the factories around them produced unprecedented wealth.

1780
Steam engines enter factories. Productivity begins to climb.
1780–1840
The Engels' Pause. Output soars. Real wages stagnate. All gains flow to capital owners.
1840–1900
Labour organising, political reform, and public education force gains to be shared broadly.
2023–?
AI enters knowledge work. Productivity climbs. The distributional question is wide open.

Are we heading for a cognitive Engels' Pause? The early signals are mixed. Workers with AI skills command higher wages. But entry-level positions, the on-ramp every previous generation climbed, are the roles most amenable to automation. A generation risks losing the bottom rungs of the ladder while being told the ladder still works.

If the Industrial Revolution teaches us anything, it's that the technology doesn't determine whether the outcome is broadly prosperous or brutally unequal. Institutions determine that. Education determines that. And the choices made by individual leaders, at the level of a single organisation, determine it too.

The engine doesn't have a moral compass. The operator does.

IXThe Calculus of Nations

The Industrial Revolution rearranged the geopolitical order for two centuries. Britain's coal advantage became economic supremacy. Not because of military genius. Because of energy economics.

But here the analogy breaks. And it breaks in a direction that should give us hope.

Coal was geographically fixed. Tokens are not. Anyone with an internet connection can access the same AI systems, at the same prices, as anyone else on the planet. The cognitive fuel is globally distributed in a way that physical fuel never was. Never could be.

The constraint is no longer access to the engine. It's the ability to operate it. Which means the advantage flows to societies and organisations that invest in human capability. A small organisation that masters AI-augmented workflows can outperform a large one that hasn't. A lean team with a token budget and the skills to use it can compete with institutions many times its size.

You don't need to be a technology company. You don't need a research department. You need to understand that your people, plus fuel, can produce what used to require an army.

XWhat Watt Would Tell You

James Watt didn't just invent a unit of measurement. He invented a way of thinking. Before horsepower, decisions were made on instinct. After horsepower, people could calculate, compare, and plan. The unit turned the steam engine from a curiosity into an irrefutable business case.

Agentpower is an invitation to do the same.

Start by asking: what is our AP ratio? Then ask: what would it cost to move it to 2.0? Then ask the harder question: what happens if our competition reaches 3.0 while we're still at 1.0?

I don't write this to frighten. I write it because the honest naming of a structural shift is a kindness, not a threat. The shift is here. It is measurable. And for the first time in the history of industrial revolutions, the fuel is cheap enough and accessible enough that size and geography are no longer destiny.

What's not equally distributed is understanding.

The brewers in 1782 didn't need to understand thermodynamics. They needed someone to say: twenty horsepower, gentlemen. That's what this machine does.

So let me say it plainly. Your people, plus a few thousand dollars a year in token fuel, can produce the cognitive output of a team several times their size. The unit is agentpower. The economics are real. The only question is whether you'll be the brewery that bought the engine, or the one that kept the horses.

The brewers who bought the engines are the reason you've heard of Birmingham. The ones who kept the horses? Nobody remembers their names.

EUR/AP: Pricing Agentpower Across Models

A proposed methodology for calculating the euro cost of one additional agentpower, beyond the human baseline, across major AI providers.

Step 1 · Define the Standard Cognitive Workday (SCW)

Before we can price one AP, we need to define how many tokens a knowledge worker consumes per day when operating in an AI-augmented workflow. We propose the Standard Cognitive Workday as the reference unit: the token mix consumed in one productive 8-hour day of AI-augmented knowledge work.

SCW Token Profile (per day): Input tokens: 60,000 (prompts, documents fed in, context) Cached tokens: 90,000 (repeated context, system prompts, project files) Output tokens: 20,000 (drafts, analyses, code, responses generated) Reasoning tokens: 30,000 (chain-of-thought for complex tasks, where applicable) Total: 200,000 tokens/day weighted equivalent

This profile reflects a worker who uses AI throughout the day across varied tasks: drafting, analysis, research, review, and communication. It accounts for real-world prompt patterns where input and cached context heavily outweigh output, and reasoning models are used selectively for complex problems.

Step 2 · Calculate the Annual Token Budget (ATB)

One additional AP of output requires one year of sustained AI-augmented work. At 250 working days per year:

Annual Token Budget per additional AP: Input: 60,000 × 250 = 15,000,000 input tokens Cached: 90,000 × 250 = 22,500,000 cached tokens Output: 20,000 × 250 = 5,000,000 output tokens Reasoning: 30,000 × 250 = 7,500,000 reasoning tokens

Step 3 · Apply Model Pricing

Multiply each token class by the model's published rate per million tokens, then sum. This gives the USD/AP: the annual cost of one additional agentpower from a given model.

USD/AP = (Input × rate) + (Cached × rate) + (Output × rate) + (Reasoning × rate) where rates are expressed as $/million tokens and token counts are the ATB values from Step 2

What EUR/AP Tells You

At €28–€120 per additional AP for mid-tier models, the marginal cost of one year of additional cognitive output is less than a single day's salary in most EU economies. Even at premium model pricing (€550+/AP), the cost remains a fraction of hiring an additional full-time employee.

The methodology is intentionally conservative. It assumes standard-intensity use, not agent-heavy or code-generation-heavy workflows that would consume more tokens. Real-world USD/AP will vary by profession, task mix, and operator skill. The framework provides a common basis for comparison.

As token prices continue to fall, USD/AP will decrease, further widening the gap between AI-augmented and unaided knowledge work economics.
